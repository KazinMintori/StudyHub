Roll a pair of fair six-sided dice and let 
[MATH] \(X = 1\) if the dice land showing the same number of spots, and 
[MATH] \(0\) otherwise. For example, if both dice land 
[MATH] \(2\), then 
[MATH] \(X = 1\), but if one lands 
[MATH] \(2\) and the other lands 
[MATH] \(3\), then 
[MATH] \(X = 0\).

What is 
[MATH] \(P(X=1)\)?

The chance that 
[MATH] \(X = 1\) can be broken up into six mutually exclusive situations, that the dice both show one spot, or both show two spots, etc. Each of these has probability 
[MATH] \(1/36\) so the total probability by the addition rule is 
[MATH] \(6/36 = 1/6\)

You have 
[MATH] \(10\) people with a cold and you have a remedy with a 
[MATH] \(20\%\) chance of success. What is the chance that your remedy will cure at least one sufferer? (Let 
[MATH] \(X\) be the number of people cured among the 10. We are looking for the probability that 
[MATH] \(X \ge 1\))

What is the chance that at least one person is cured?

[MATH] \(P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X < 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - (1-p)^{10}\), where 
[MATH] \(p = 0.2\), because 
[MATH] \(X \sim Bin(10, p)\). 
[MATH] \(1-p = 0.8 \: \& \: 1-0.8^{10} = 0.8926\)
