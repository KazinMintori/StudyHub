[MATH] \(T\) is a random variable with the distribution shown below:

[MATH] \[
T = \begin{cases}
3, \; \text{ with prob } 1/3\\
4, \; \text{ with prob } 1/4\\
5, \; \text{ with prob } 5/12
\end{cases}
\]

Consider the box with tickets: 
[MATH] \(\fbox{3}\, \fbox{3}\, \fbox{3} \,\fbox{4}  \,\fbox{4} \,\fbox{4} \,\fbox{4} \,\fbox{5} \,\fbox{5}\, \fbox{5} \,\fbox{5} \,\fbox{5}\)

Suppose we draw once from this box and let 
[MATH] \(U\) be the value of the ticket drawn. Which random variable has a higher expected value?

The expected value of 
[MATH] \(T\) is ____ the expected value of 
[MATH] \(U\).

This is a reading question. Note that the pmf implied by the box is not the same as the given pmf. In the box, 4 has a higher probability, so the average is higher. They do not need to actually do the computation. At this point, ask them what box would they use so that if 
[MATH] \(T\) is the value of a randomly selected ticket, the distribution of 
[MATH] \(T\) is exactly the distribution shown here.

A prof notices that Zoom office hours are not too crowded this semester. She observes that a random variable 
[MATH] \(X\) representing the number of Stat 20 students coming to her Thursday office hours has a Poisson(2) distribution. Furthermore, there is one Data 88 student from a previous semester who is always there (they want a letter of recommendation).

Let 
[MATH] \(V\) be the total number of students in her Zoom office hours. What is the expected value and variance of 
[MATH] \(V\)?

This is also a reading question, with < 50% getting it right. Let 
[MATH] \(X\) be the number of Stat 20 students who go to the office hours. Then 
[MATH] \(E(X) = 2 = Var(X)\). But the number of students in the office is 
[MATH] \(X+1\) since that one student is always there. 
[MATH] \(E(X+1) = 2+1 = 3\) and 
[MATH] \(Var(X+1) = Var(X) = 2\)
